Civilian jobs to research after serving as a Navy ET

The Navy describes ET duties involving communications, radar, navigation, identification equipment, and calibration of test equipment. This guide focuses on ET electronics experience; nuclear, aviation, and other specialties can have different training and duties. Your rating is a starting point for research, not proof that you meet every civilian technical requirement. [1]

The following roles are suggested matches based on overlapping work. Compare the actual equipment, work environment, education, and technical depth of each opening with your experience.

Electronics repair technician

Consider this direction if you diagnosed faults, interpreted technical information, checked components or assemblies, and verified equipment after repairs. Describe the level at which you worked and the instruments you actually used.

Requirements to compare: Requirements vary by industry and equipment. An electronics technology degree or technical training may be valued, but the posting decides what is required. Experience replacing assemblies does not establish board-level repair or soldering proficiency. [2]

Field service technician

This may fit troubleshooting, scheduled maintenance, repair documentation, and explaining equipment status to users. It can suit ETs who want hands-on technical work at customer or operating sites.

Requirements to compare: Check travel, driving, on-call, physical, and customer-support requirements. Industrial, laboratory, or medical equipment can require manufacturer-specific training. Navy equipment experience does not automatically qualify you to service an unfamiliar product.

Electronics test technician

A useful direction when you set up test equipment, followed test procedures, recorded results, and investigated failures. Explain how you checked results against an approved limit or specification.

Requirements to compare: Compare the employer’s instruments, documentation practices, automation tools, and product domain. Following a test procedure is different from designing the test or owning an engineering verification program. Identify which parts you performed.

RF or communications equipment technician

Consider this path if your work involved communications equipment, signal checks, antennas, or fault isolation in a communications chain. Use releasable examples of troubleshooting and equipment restoration.

Requirements to compare: Check the specific radio-frequency equipment, installation work, cabling, test methods, and any role-specific authorizations. Shipboard equipment maintenance is not automatically equivalent to commercial carrier network design or every field-installation task.

Calibration technician

Investigate calibration roles when you actually compared measurement devices with standards, recorded results, and performed authorized adjustments. If you mainly tracked due dates or routed instruments to a lab, describe calibration coordination instead.

Requirements to compare: Calibration occupations typically involve technical education or training, with some on-the-job entry routes. Employers may require particular measurement disciplines, lab procedures, or credentials. Instrument use alone does not establish calibration competence. [3]

Systems integration technician

This may fit experience installing equipment, connecting interfaces, checking system functions, and documenting discrepancies during installation or maintenance. Explain how you verified that components worked together.

Requirements to compare: Review requirements for drawings, configuration records, software tools, and acceptance testing. Technical installation work should not be presented as systems architecture, software engineering, or design authority unless your experience supports those claims.

Illustrative Navy ET resume bullets

Keep Electronics Technician and Navy service in your work history. Add a truthful focus, such as communications equipment maintenance or electronic testing, if it helps a civilian employer understand the assignment.

  • Diagnosed faults in [equipment category] using [actual instruments] and technical procedures, documenting findings and corrective actions.
  • Completed [number] scheduled maintenance tasks during [period], recording equipment condition and post-maintenance verification results.
  • Executed [specific test procedure], compared readings with approved limits, and escalated out-of-tolerance results to [responsible role].

Check qualifications before choosing training

Separate technician work from engineering qualifications

Electronics engineering technologist and technician roles typically call for an associate degree, with employer-specific variations. Formal electrical and electronics engineer roles typically require a related bachelor’s degree. A similar-sounding job title does not make those requirements interchangeable. [2] [4]

Choose your target by the daily duties and required technical depth. Technical specialist or technician openings may provide a closer match than a design-engineering role when your experience centers on maintenance, testing, or installation.

Fill a specific equipment or measurement gap

Compare current postings for the instruments, product family, software, and documentation skills they request. Prioritize a gap that appears repeatedly instead of collecting unrelated certifications.

Review manufacturer training, relevant electronics education, or metrology training when it fits the target role. Verify current eligibility and employer expectations before paying for a credential. For defense openings, check technical qualifications and clearance requirements separately.

Navy ET civilian career questions

What civilian jobs can a Navy ET pursue?

Possible directions include electronics repair, electronic testing, field service, communications equipment support, and selected calibration or integration roles. Match the opening to the equipment and technical work you actually performed.

Does Navy ET experience qualify me as an electrical engineer?

Maintenance and testing experience can be relevant, but formal electrical and electronics engineering positions typically require a related bachelor’s degree. Technician and technologist roles have different entry routes. Review the specific job requirements. [4]

Are Navy ET and Navy IT civilian career paths the same?

There can be overlap around equipment and communications, but the ratings cover different work. Use evidence of electronics maintenance, network support, systems administration, or other functions rather than treating the rating names as equivalent.

Can I claim calibration experience if I tracked calibration dates?

Describe the tracking and coordination you performed. Calibration bench work involves different measurement tasks. Claim it only when you actually performed the relevant comparisons, adjustments, and documentation.

Does a security clearance guarantee a defense job?

No. Read the opening’s clearance language and confirm your status with the appropriate security contact. You also need the technical, education, and other qualifications the employer requires.

Can I apply if I did not do board-level repairs?

Look for jobs that match your actual repair level, such as assembly replacement, equipment troubleshooting, maintenance, or testing. Do not claim soldering or component-level repair proficiency you have not developed.
