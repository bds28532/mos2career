Turn a guide into a focused job search

  1. Choose work you can demonstrate. List the systems, duties, responsibilities, and tools from your own assignments. A general specialty description may include work you never performed.
  2. Compare two or three real openings. Read their duties, education, experience, credentials, and location requirements. Separate qualifications you hold from those you still need to verify.
  3. Write evidence, then choose a next step. Adapt resume examples with accurate details and replace every bracketed placeholder. Decide whether your next step is an application, a skills project, or a specific credential check.

For healthcare roles, military medical experience does not automatically authorize civilian clinical practice. Verify the current requirements with the relevant state authority, credentialing body, and employer. For technical and logistics roles, check the specific tools and level of responsibility requested.

Choosing a civilian career guide

Does a suggested civilian job mean I already qualify?

No. The guides identify directions worth researching. Employers determine hiring qualifications, and regulated work may require separate education, examinations, credentials, or state authorization. Verify the requirements of the actual opening.

Can I use a guide from another service?

You can compare similar functions, such as inventory, IT support, or equipment maintenance, while keeping your official military specialty and actual duties accurate. Similar duties do not establish equivalent training or professional credentials.

What if I want a different type of work?

Start with the strengths you want to keep using, such as troubleshooting, training, coordination, or documentation. Use the free Career Translator to explore other directions, then compare current job postings and identify specific gaps.
