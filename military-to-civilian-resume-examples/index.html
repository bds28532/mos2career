Military to Civilian Resume Examples

You know the work you did in uniform. A civilian employer needs to understand what you maintained, who you supported, and how you solved problems. Start with these before-and-after examples, then write a version that matches your own experience and the job you want.

How to translate military experience on a resume

Use this structure: action + specific work + scope or verified result. A useful bullet makes your contribution clear even when the reader has never heard of your unit, rating, MOS, or AFSC.

  1. Choose a duty from the job posting. For a maintenance opening, start with a relevant repair or inspection. For a support opening, start with a user problem you resolved.
  2. Describe what you personally did. Name the equipment, process, customer, or team at a level you can discuss publicly. Use the employer’s terminology only when it accurately describes your work.
  3. Add evidence you can explain. A verified team size, workload, turnaround time, or inspection result adds context. If you cannot support a number, describe the completed work without one.

The VA’s resume guidance recommends explaining your actual duties in familiar language and using the job announcement to understand an employer’s terminology. [1] The examples below apply that approach to fictional scenarios.

Six military to civilian resume bullet examples

Read the civilian version as a writing pattern. Change the duty, tools, and outcome to match your records. Keep your actual military job title in your work history; a plain-language description can help explain its function.

Navy Fire Controlman: electronics maintenance

Before: Performed PMS and corrective maintenance on assigned FC equipment.

Civilian version: Diagnosed electronic equipment faults using technical manuals and test equipment, completed repairs, and documented verification checks before returning equipment to service.

Make it yours: Specify a releasable equipment category and the tests you actually performed. If you assisted with repairs, describe that support rather than claiming independent repair responsibility.

Navy Fire Controlman: technical training

Before: Trained junior sailors on maintenance procedures and qualification requirements.

Civilian version: Guided junior technicians through equipment inspection and maintenance procedures, demonstrated troubleshooting steps, and checked their work against documented requirements.

Make it yours: Explain whether you delivered formal instruction, coached coworkers, or evaluated work. Add a verified number of trainees only if you can support it.

Relevant evidence: Training records, approved lesson material, or documented responsibilities can help you prepare an interview example.

Army 25B: user and workstation support

Before: Supported S6 operations and resolved user trouble calls.

Civilian version: Troubleshot workstation, application, and connectivity issues for unit personnel, recorded the steps taken, and escalated unresolved problems to the appropriate technical team.

Make it yours: Name operating systems or ticketing tools only if you used them. If the opening emphasizes customer service, explain how you communicated updates to users.

Navy IT: communications support

Before: Stood communications watch and maintained system logs.

Civilian version: Monitored communications services during assigned shifts, documented service interruptions, and coordinated troubleshooting and shift handovers to maintain continuity of support.

Make it yours: Identify the service you supported and your level of responsibility. Monitoring a system and administering that system are different tasks; describe the work you performed.

Navy LS: inventory and supply

Before: Managed repair parts, conducted inventories, and processed requisitions.

Civilian version: Reconciled physical stock with inventory records, researched discrepancies, and tracked parts requests and deliveries to support maintenance teams.

Make it yours: Describe the inventory system and the steps you handled. Do not turn purchasing support into a claim that you held procurement authority.

Any branch: team coordination

Before: Led a section and ensured mission readiness.

Civilian version: Assigned daily work, tracked task completion, and coordinated equipment and staffing needs so the team could complete scheduled activities.

Make it yours: Explain the kind of work and your decision-making responsibility. Add the verified team size, schedule, or completed deliverable to give the reader context.

Relevant evidence: A specific example of scheduling, resolving a work conflict, or preparing a handover is easier to discuss than a broad claim about leadership.

A simple military to civilian resume template

Use this outline for a work-history entry. Replace every bracketed field before applying. Keep only the skills and credentials you have; the target job title belongs in your career goal, not in place of a job you never held.

[Actual military job title] — [plain-language function]
[Service / organization] | [Month Year–Month Year]

• [Action verb] [specific task or problem] using [tools or process you used].
• [Action verb] [work you owned] for [verified scope], resulting in [supported outcome].
• [Action verb] [team, customer, or training contribution] with [relevant context].

Relevant training and credentials
[Exact credential or course name] | [Issuer] | [Date / current status]

For example, “Navy Fire Controlman — Electronics Maintenance” can explain a function without changing the official rating. Choose a description that reflects your assignment. A course completion and an active professional certification should each be labeled accurately.

Check your wording before you apply

  • Relevance: Can the reader connect each selected bullet to a duty or qualification in this opening?
  • Clarity: Have you explained acronyms and described the work in words a civilian reader can follow?
  • Evidence: Can you explain every tool, credential, number, and result during an interview?
  • Scope: Is it clear whether you performed, supported, supervised, or approved the work?
  • Release: Does the resume contain only information you are authorized to share publicly?

For the full application, follow the employer’s current instructions about format and required information. This worksheet helps you develop work-history language; it does not replace those instructions.
